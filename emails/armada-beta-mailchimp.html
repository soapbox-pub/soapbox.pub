Armada: Encrypted Discord Alternative Built on Nostr

By M. K. Fain  ·  July 30, 2026

Armada is now in open beta: a free, open-source, end-to-end encrypted alternative to Discord. Servers, channels, threads, voice and video, screen sharing, roles, and moderation, with no company in the middle and no server that can be seized or switched off.

You can install it right now from Google Play and Zap Store, or open it in any browser as a PWA at armada.buzz. iOS is coming soon.

Discord is cracking down

Over the past year Discord has rolled out ID and face-scan age checks, handed moderation to AI that bans accounts faster than anyone can appeal, and leaked users' government-ID photos in a third-party breach.

Other Discord alternatives exist, and none of them go far enough. Stoat and Fluxer are friendly open-source clones, but they leave your chat unencrypted on one company's servers; Matrix is a chore to run and moderate. We put the whole field side by side in The Best Discord Alternatives in 2026. Armada is the only one that is end-to-end encrypted by default and has no company that can shut it down.

Familiar on the surface. End-to-end encrypted underneath.

Discord's features, without Discord

Everything you expect from a community chat app, with the ownership flipped:

  • Servers, channels, and threads, laid out the way your community already knows
  • Encrypted voice and video calls with screen sharing
  • Roles, permissions, invites, and moderation tools
  • Encrypted DMs that live right next to your communities
  • Android, Windows, Linux, and the web today, with iOS on the way
  • Open protocols underneath: also speaks Nostr's NIP-29 and connects to Block's Buzz workspaces

No company in the middle

Every community is end-to-end encrypted by default, using the open Concord protocol. Messages are sealed on your device and published as ordinary Nostr events, so the relays that carry them store ciphertext they cannot read, and neither can we.

An account is a cryptographic key. There is no email, no phone number, and no ID check to sign up, so there is no honeypot of personal data to breach and nothing to hand over under subpoena. You cannot be de-platformed from a network that has no platform.

Run the whole thing yourself

Communities are serverless by default, so most people never host anything. But if you want chat as internal infrastructure, Armada ships a complete open-source stack: the web app, the relay, and the voice server, all AGPL-3.0. One command brings it up on your own hardware, air-gapped if you want.
